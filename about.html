About

I am an AI researcher working across reinforcement learning, multimodal learning, and sequential decision-making. I am currently a Founding Research Scientist at Trapezoid AI, where I lead applied ML research on model adaptation, perception, and agentic systems.

Research background

My Ph.D. research at the University of Washington focused on how people and autonomous systems make decisions over time. I developed offline and inverse reinforcement learning models for driver behavior and adaptive decisions, using VR simulations and human-subject experiments to study driver–automation interactions under distraction and neurocognitive variability.

This work brought together machine learning, simulation, and behavioral research through collaborations with Toyota CSRC, DENSO, and NIH-funded studies. My subsequent applied ML work has included multimodal safety systems, healthcare, and production agentic perception.

Research communication and service

I won first place in the University of Washington’s 2023 Three Minute Thesis competition, presenting research on sequential decision-making in driving. Read the UW profile.

I am a selected member of the Transportation Research Board’s ACP-70 committee. My recognitions include the UW Diversity, Equity & Inclusion Award (2024), a Grace Hopper Scholarship (2021), and a Data Science for Social Good fellowship (2017).
